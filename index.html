What your GPU can actually do

The single most demoralising beginner mistake is attempting a run that cannot fit, then concluding you are bad at this. Here is the honest ceiling, assuming bf16 and sensible batch sizes. "Train from scratch" means pretraining your own weights; "QLoRA" means 4-bit base + low-rank adapters.

Rented 8× H1007–13 B *≤ 70 Bany open modelany open model

Rough rule for training memory: weights + gradients + Adam's two moments ≈ 16 bytes per parameter in mixed precision, before a single activation is stored. A 1 B model therefore wants ~16 GB just to hold its own training state — which is why 24 GB tops out near 700 M for comfortable from-scratch work, why an 8-bit optimiser plus gradient checkpointing roughly doubles what fits, and why LoRA (training ~0.1% of the parameters) changes the arithmetic completely. * The 8× H100 from-scratch figure is what fits in memory, not what fits in a budget: pretraining a 7 B model properly means trillions of tokens and weeks of rented time. Only two things on this roadmap actually need rented GPUs — the stage-4 nanochat run (~$100) and one multi-GPU FSDP experiment in stage 5 (~$30).

Habits that decide whether this works

The syllabus is the easy part. Almost everyone who fails at an 18-month self-taught track fails on these instead.

  • Implement before you import. Write it badly from scratch once — then use the library forever. Reading about backprop teaches you nothing; a wrong gradient you had to hunt down teaches you everything.
  • Maths on paper, in parallel. Never batch six weeks of pure maths before touching code. Two hours of maths and two of code in the same week make each other stick.
  • Overfit one batch first. Before any real training run, prove the model can memorise 8 examples. If it can't, the bug is in your code, not your hyperparameters.
  • Keep a lab notebook. One file per experiment: hypothesis, config, result, what surprised you. In month 14 this is the difference between having learned and having watched.
  • Ship publicly every stage. A repo, a write-up, a model card. Explaining it is how you find out what you don't understand.
  • Budget compute deliberately. Decide the run's cost before launching it. "I'll just leave it training" is how side projects die.
  • Read one paper a week from stage 3 onward. Non-negotiable and cumulative — about 60 papers by the end, which is roughly the working canon.
  • Expect the wall around month 5. It comes for everyone, usually mid-stage-3. Drop the hours, don't drop the streak.

What to deliberately skip

A roadmap is as much about exclusion as inclusion. These will be recommended to you constantly and will cost you months.

  • Measure-theoretic probability. Beautiful, and irrelevant to everything below stage 9. Undergraduate probability is genuinely enough.
  • A full maths degree before any code. The single most common way this track dies. You need working fluency, not proofs.
  • TensorFlow / Keras. Research and open-weight tooling standardised on PyTorch. Learn one framework deeply.
  • Tutorial-hopping. Fifteen half-finished Colab notebooks teach less than one finished ugly project.
  • Pretraining a "real" LLM. A 7 B model from scratch costs six figures. Your from-scratch model is 10–500 M parameters, and that teaches the same lessons.
  • Waiting to feel ready. You will start stage 4 feeling shaky about stage 3. That's the normal state of everyone doing this.

The short list

One or two per stage, chosen because they are complete and free or cheap — not an exhaustive link dump. Everything here was live as of September 2026.

Stage 0–1 · Foundations

  • Automate the Boring StuffFree book. Python for people who have never programmed.
  • 3Blue1Brown · Essence of Linear AlgebraWatch before any textbook. Builds the geometric intuition everything else assumes.
  • Mathematics for Machine LearningFree PDF. The reference for exactly the maths on this roadmap and nothing more.
  • NumPy · absolute beginnersBroadcasting and axis semantics are the whole game.

Stage 2–3 · ML & deep learning

  • Karpathy · Neural Networks: Zero to HeroThe centre of gravity of this entire roadmap. Build micrograd, then makemore, then GPT.
  • Dive into Deep LearningFree, interactive, PyTorch. The best single reference text.
  • fast.ai · Practical Deep LearningTop-down counterweight. Gets you results early, which sustains morale.
  • An Introduction to Statistical LearningFree PDF. Classical ML done properly.

Stage 4–6 · LLMs

  • Raschka · Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch)Book + full repo. Tokenizer to fine-tuned chat model, line by line.
  • karpathy/nanochatThe full pipeline — pretrain, SFT, RL, web UI — for about $100 of rented GPU.
  • Stanford CS336 · Language Modeling from ScratchSpring 2026 lectures and assignments are public. The graduate-level version of stages 4–5.
  • The Illustrated TransformerRead it the day before you implement attention, not instead of implementing it.

Stage 7–9 · Applied & research

  • Hugging Face coursesNLP, agents, deep RL, diffusion — all free, all hands-on.
  • vLLM docsPaged attention and continuous batching, explained by the implementation.
  • arXiv cs.LGSkim titles weekly. Read one properly.
  • DeepLearning.AI short coursesTwo-hour applied modules — useful for filling stage-7 gaps fast.
